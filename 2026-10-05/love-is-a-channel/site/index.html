Love is a channel business

How you met predicts how long it lasts — mostly because of who arrives through each door. HCMST 2017–2022, 2,787 couples. Musing with Mike.

–

Channel scorecard (all couples, model-adjusted)

Adjusted = predicted breakup risk by 2022 if the same couples (same ages, education, marital status) had met through each channel instead. Model: XGBoost on 1,031 couples partnered in 2017 and re-interviewed in 2022; test AUC 0.59 — honest, weak. The door matters less than who's standing in it.

Data: How Couples Meet and Stay Together 2017–2022, public v2.2 (Rosenfeld et al., Stanford). Estimates are model outputs for exploration, not personal predictions. Raw Kaplan–Meier survival tells a harsher story about online (74.7% together at 5 years vs. 92.0% for friends) — that gap is mostly composition: online couples in this sample are older at the start (34.5 vs. 25.6), far less often married (36% vs. 67%), and more often in newer, untested relationships.
