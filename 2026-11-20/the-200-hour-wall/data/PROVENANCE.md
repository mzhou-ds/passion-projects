# Provenance

- ATUS diary-day extract: `../../2026-11-02/the-friendless-day/data/respondent_day.csv`
  (250,165 diary days, 2003-2025, U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics American
  Time Use Survey microdata: Activity, Who, Respondent, Roster files).
  Columns used here: `era`, `ageband`, `TUFINLWGT` (survey weight),
  `friend_min` (nonwork minutes with a friend present). The 2026-11-02
  build's PROVENANCE.md documents the BLS download; the raw .dat files
  (~1 GB) are not committed. This build recomputes the weighted age
  curve from that extract when present, and otherwise falls back to the
  committed aggregate `data/age_friend_hours.csv`, which reproduces every
  headline number on its own.
- Hall (2019) thresholds: pooled 50 / 90 / 200+ hours; adult-only
  94 / 164 / ~264 hours; student 43 / ~100 / ~119 hours. Source paper:
  Hall, J. A. (2019), "How many hours does it take to make a friend?",
  Journal of Social and Personal Relationships 36(4), 1278-1296,
  DOI 10.1177/0265407518761225. Adult/student splits as summarized by
  Greater Good Science Center (UC Berkeley), "How Long Does It Take to
  Make a Friend?", which also reports the concentration mechanism
  (students who upgraded a friendship doubled hours with that person
  and halved time with others) and the relaxed-time-only finding.
- Derived outputs in this folder (`age_friend_hours.csv`,
  `years_to_friend.csv`, `cadence.csv`, `results.json`) are generated by
  `src/01_analysis.py`.
