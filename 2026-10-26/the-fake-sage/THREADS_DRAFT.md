I counted every quote Wikiquote has for 28 philosophers and sorted them by whether anyone can actually source them. 4,253 quotes in all.

Laozi has the worst fake rate in the canon. 19% of his checkable quotes are disputed or misattributed. He is also the most read philosopher in the set, 4.6M Wikipedia views in 2024, ahead of Marcus Aurelius and Nietzsche.

The fakes are not random, though. That is the part I did not expect. Fake quotes run 15 words at the median against 34 for real ones. They use therapy words at more than twice the rate. They talk to you, directly, two and a half times as often. A classifier can tell fake from real by voice alone, no author name, at an AUC of 0.74.

And the voice gets aimed. Fake quotes signed by Eastern sages carry two and a half times the therapy vocabulary of fake quotes signed by Western thinkers. Their real writings are nowhere near that far apart. When the feed needs a line about healing and your inner light, it does not sign it Kant. It signs it Rumi.

Rumi is his own case. 84% of his quotes on Wikiquote have no source text at all. Not proven fake. Just floating, with no receipt anyone can check. That gap is where the real fakes hide.

My favorite detail: the most famous Confucius line on the internet, the journey of a thousand miles, is filed under his misattributed quotes. It is from the Tao Te Ching. The fake did not die when it got caught. It just changed sages.
