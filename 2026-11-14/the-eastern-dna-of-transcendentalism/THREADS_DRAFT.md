Thoreau called the Bhagavad Gita "stupendous and cosmogonal." He said he bathed his intellect in it every morning.

Then I counted. Across all 107,807 words of Walden, there is not one sentence that shares six words in a row with the Gita. Zero.

The Bible shows up 18 times. The Vishnu Purana twice. The Gita never.

He names the East as often as the West. He just never borrows its sentences. He quotes the book he argues with and paraphrases the one he lives by.

Emerson does the mirror image. He names the Bible eight times as often as the East, but his vocabulary leans Gita over Bible in all twelve essays.

Citations are politics. Wording is DNA. Full build below.
