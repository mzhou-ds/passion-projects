I spent the week inside 13,882 NHL games, every play since 2015, chasing one question. How much of the scoring boom is just empty nets?

Short answer: barely any of it. Goals per game went from 5.4 to 6.3 over the decade. Empty netters explain about a tenth of that. The boom is real goals past real goalies.

The wilder finding is what 6-on-5 actually is. A team with its goalie pulled scores 7.8 goals per 60 minutes. A power play scores 7.0. Same extra skater. The only difference is the goalie. The goalie is worth more than the man advantage.

But it costs you. While your net is empty, the other team scores at 21.6 per 60. Almost three times as fast as you do. Pull down two goals and you tie 1.6% of the time and give one up 52% of the time.

And coaches are still late. The median pull is 1:48 left. The math says anywhere inside seven and a half minutes beats staying 5-on-5. Pull at two to three minutes and you tie 18% of the time. Pull under a minute and it is 6%.

One more, for the goal race people. In 2024-25 Ovechkin scored 8 empty netters, 18% of his goals. Draisaitl won the Rocket that year with 52 and only 2 were empty netters. This past season Cole Caufield scored 51 with zero.
