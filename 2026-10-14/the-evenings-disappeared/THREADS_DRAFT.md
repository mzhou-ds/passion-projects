The friendship recession story is wrong, and I can show you the chart.

I ran the General Social Survey year by year, 1974 to 2024. That is 45,294 adults answering how often they spend a social evening with friends.

From 1974 to 2018 the number does not move. About 65% of adults saw friends socially at least once a month, every survey, for 44 years. No slow slide. No bowling alone decay curve.

Then it breaks. 2014-2018: 64.6%. 2022-2024: 56.8%. The share who never spend an evening with friends went from 8.5% to 12.7%.

And nobody traded down to family. Evenings with relatives fell even faster in volume. Everything fell at once.

It is not demographics either. I split the drop across 132 life situations (age, married or not, kids, job). Changing who Americans are explains 0.6 of the 7.8 points. The rest happens inside identical lives. A married 40-year-old with kids and a job in 2024 just sees friends less than that exact person did in 2016.

The hit landed on the young and the single. Ages 25-34 lost 11 points. Never-married adults lost 14. People over 65 lost 2.

Friend evenings still price into happiness, about an 8 to 9 point adjusted gap in "very happy" between weekly friend time and almost none. That gap held in both eras. The returns did not change. The attendance did.

My read: evenings survive on defaults, not intentions. The standing game, the recurring dinner, the thing on the calendar that you opt out of instead of opt into. Everything else was discretionary, and discretionary is what got cut.
