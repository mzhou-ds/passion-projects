Your $499 longevity blood panel grades you "optimal" or not on a hundred markers. I ran the lab-medicine numbers on 14 of them, and the flagship marker fails its own premise.

hsCRP is the inflammation number every longevity panel sells you on. Its day-to-day variation inside one person is 59%. The "optimal" line is drawn at 0.5. If your true value is 0.4, comfortably optimal, one blood draw still tells you you're above the line a third of the time. And for your hsCRP to show a real change between two tests, it has to nearly triple.

It gets worse in aggregate. A person who is dead-centre optimal on all 14 markers still has a 69% chance that at least one comes back mislabelled. The panel is mostly noise wearing a verdict's clothes.

Not all of it, though. ApoB, HbA1c, ferritin, homocysteine: one draw grades those fine. The lesson isn't "don't test." It's that the markers worth paying to track are the boring cheap ones, and the premium price is built around the one that can't be measured precisely enough to grade.
