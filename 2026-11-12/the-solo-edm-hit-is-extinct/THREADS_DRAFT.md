The last time a DJ hit the Billboard top 40 alone was 2017. The Chainsmokers, "Paris." Since then, five solo DJ records have charted at all. Best peak: No. 57.

I went through every Hot 100 chart from 2010 to now, 88,100 slots, and read the billing on all 192 dance songs. The solo DJ did not fade out. The chart started charging a toll in 2015, and the toll was a pop singer on the credit line.

In 2014 solo DJs still held 39% of dance music's chart slots. In 2015 the share was zero. Same year "Lean On" hit No. 4 and "Where Are U Now" hit No. 8. Every one of them paid the toll.

And it is not just "pop got more collaborative." The whole chart's collab rate barely moved, 34% to 37%. EDM's went 74% to 99%. The gap never dropped below 28 points in any year.

The twist: solo records were never worse. In the boom years they hit the top 40 exactly as often as collabs did. The gate moved, not the hit rate.

Today the remnant is two men. Marshmello and Guetta hold 62% of dance music's entire Hot 100 presence since 2021. Nineteen EDM acts charted in 2018. Three charted in 2025.
