The Friendship Budget

Friendships have maintenance costs, just like investments have fees. Robin Dunbar's research says humans can sustain about 150 meaningful relationships, in layers of roughly 5 · 15 · 50 · 150 — but each layer bills you in hours. The takeaway from the data

In 1990, 55% of American men had six or more close friends; by 2021 it was 27%, and the share with no close friends quintupled from 3% to 15% (AEI Survey Center on American Life, 2021). Meanwhile the World Happiness Report 2025 finds 19% of young adults worldwide have no one to count on — up 39% since 2006. The model above shows why the math is unforgiving: an inner circle of five alone costs ~20 hours a month. Friendship isn't free — it's the highest-return asset most of us systematically underfund. Strong social ties carry a survival advantage (OR ≈ 1.5) comparable to the mortality risk of smoking (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010).

Play with the presets: The average American mirrors AEI's 2021 distribution (most people hold 1–3 close friends and little else). Inner-circle investor concentrates the budget where the evidence says returns are highest. The networker spreads thin — watch the inner circle starve.
