# The Friend Workout Died After 2020 — America exercises more than ever, and alone

**Musing with Mike · daily build #51 · 2026-10-27 · topic: fitness**

Every fitness headline of the last decade points the same way: group classes, CrossFit boxes, run clubs, Barry's, F45. The industry spent ten years selling community, and by its own numbers it worked. Participation is up, minutes are up, gym memberships are up.

The time diaries tell a different story about what actually happened inside those workouts. From the American Time Use Survey (258,954 diary days, 2003–2025, 48,258 exercise episodes with a recorded companion), I measured something the industry stats never report: not how much America exercises, but *who was in the room*. The answer is that exercise did not gradually go solo over twenty years of phones and Pelotons. It held its company for seventeen years, then lost it all at once after 2020, and what died was specifically the friend.

**Try it:** open [`explorer.html`](explorer.html) — pick your workout and your age band and see your solo share, then vs now, and what company does to your session length.

## Findings

**1. The solo shift is a cliff, not a creep, and it dates to 2021.** The share of exercise minutes Americans do alone sat in a 46–53% band every single year from 2003 to 2019 (era averages: 50.8% in 2003–07, 49.1% in 2013–19). Then it jumped to 57.7% in 2021 and has stayed elevated every year since: 55.8, 54.9, 58.2, 53.7. Era 2021–25 averages **56.0% (SE 1.0)** against 50.8% (SE 0.9) in 2003–07, a +5.2 point break at roughly four standard errors. Two decades of smartphones produced no trend at all. The break is a pandemic-era break, and five years on it has not healed. See `charts/01_alone_trend.png`.

**2. What died is the friend. The spouse workout survived.** Split the company and the story sharpens. Minutes exercised with friends: 12.8% of all exercise minutes in 2003–07, 12.3% in 2013–19, then **7.6% in 2021–25**, nearly cut in half. Minutes with a spouse or partner: 15.7%, then 16.3%, then 16.7% — flat to up. Family company eased (19.7% → 16.2%). America did not stop exercising with people. It stopped exercising with *friends*, the one companion you have to schedule, while the companion who lives in your house held. The same collapse shows up inside team sports, where friend minutes fell from 48.3% to 35.2% and alone minutes doubled (7.3% → 13.7%). See `charts/02_companion_mix.png`.

**3. The young went solo. The old were always solo.** Among 15–24-year-olds, the alone share of exercise minutes went from **33.8% to 49.0%** (+15.2 points), and friend minutes fell from 28.6% to 16.9%. The 25–34 band moved +6.1 points. Every band from 45 up is flat to within a point (65+: 63.0% → 63.3%), because older exercisers were already doing most of it alone and their company was a spouse, which survived. This is not mainly a story about home gyms, which every age bought. It is a story about the workout as a social appointment disappearing from young people's weeks. Women moved more than men (+6.2 vs +4.0 points).

**4. It is not just that exercise moved home. Exercise at home itself went solo.** Home workouts rose from 22.1% to 29.0% of exercise minutes, and a Kitagawa decomposition of the +5.2 point solo rise assigns only **+1.5 points to that location shift**. The other **+3.7 points happened inside each location**: away-from-home exercise barely changed (46.2% → 47.8% alone), but exercise *at home* went from 67.0% to 75.9% alone. Even under your own roof, with your own people in it, the workout stopped being a shared activity. The gym tells the same story from the other side: gyms took a smaller share of minutes after 2020 (25.5% → 21.6%) and did not get more solo inside (63.0% → 57.7% alone), but the friend share of gym minutes halved there too (11.1% → 6.3%). See `charts/05_gym.png`.

**5. Company is the cheapest performance enhancer in the data.** Episodes with friends are the longest workouts in America: **82.6 minutes on average, against 46.9 alone** (+76%). Family company: 71.0 minutes. A spouse: 63.5. The premium is not a recent artifact; social episodes beat solo ones by about twenty minutes in every era. Causality runs both ways (a two-hour hike recruits friends; friends also keep you out longer), and I do not pretend otherwise below. But even as a selection fact it prices the loss in finding 2: the companion type that disappeared is the one attached to the longest sessions. People whose diary day included any social exercise also logged 37.4 minutes of socializing that day against 28.2 for solo exercisers, so the friend workout looks less like a workout format and more like one load-bearing piece of a social day. See `charts/03_duration.png`.

**6. More exercise, less company, at the same time.** None of this is a decline-of-exercise story. The share of Americans who exercised on their diary day rose from 13.2% (2003) to 20.5% (2025), and exercise minutes per capita rose from 9.1 to 15.0 a day. The pie grew by two-thirds while its social slice shrank. Activity by activity, the solo rise is broad: biking +14.5 points (49.7% → 64.2% alone), cardio equipment 75.5% → 81.6%, running 70.3% → 75.6%, unspecified working out 69.6% → 76.2%. Walking, the most spouse-and-family activity, is the exception that proves the rule: dead flat at ~53%. And an XGBoost model of whether an episode is solo (AUC 0.767, SHAP in `output/shap_importance.csv`) ranks location, activity type, age, sex, and hour far above calendar year: the year matters mostly through where and what the workout became, not as a force of its own. See `charts/04_by_activity.png`, `charts/06_participation.png`.

One tentative green shoot: friend minutes ticked up to 10.5% in 2025 after four years at 5.7–7.4%. One year is not a recovery, but it is the first print consistent with the run-club story actually showing up in diaries.

## The market read

The fitness industry sells community and delivers a room full of people exercising alone next to each other: about 58% of gym minutes are solo even when the building is full, and the genuinely social product, the pre-arranged friend workout, is the segment that halved. That gap is the opportunity and the warning. The warning is for subscription fitness built on social identity: your customer's actual behavior is a 47-minute solo session, and churn models built on the community story are pricing a product few people use. The opportunity is coordination, not content. What disappeared after 2020 was not motivation (participation rose) and not facilities (they reopened). It was the scheduled appointment with a friend, the exact thing a calendar nudge, a standing weekly session, or a two-person streak is built to restore, and the duration data says a restored friend is worth about twenty extra minutes a session. Whoever sells *the appointment* instead of another workout library is selling the scarce input.

## Method

- `src/download.py` — fetches the ATUS 2003–2025 multi-year Activity, Who, Respondent, and Roster zips from BLS (browser fallback noted in the file; BLS blocks some datacenter IPs).
- `src/analyze.py` — builds episodes: Activity rows with pooled six-digit codes 130100–130199, joined to Who records (one row per companion category per episode) and to respondent age/sex (Roster, matched on line number) and final weights (TUFNWGTP). Companion classes from ATUS who codes: alone = only codes 18/19 present; friends = 54; spouse/partner = 20/21; family = household 22–30 plus nonhousehold family 40/51/52/53. Headline set is a purposeful-exercise subset (aerobics, biking, climbing, dancing, gymnastics, hiking, martial arts, running, ski/skate, cardio equipment, walking, water sports, weightlifting, working out, yoga); organized sport and the full 1301 range are robustness sets and move the same way. 2020 is excluded from every trend (collection was suspended mid-March to mid-May 2020). All shares are survey-weighted; the headline era difference carries respondent-clustered standard errors. The location decomposition is a two-fold Kitagawa split.
- `src/model.py` — XGBoost (400 trees, depth 3) predicting a solo episode from year, hour, weekend, age, sex, home/gym location, and activity, with SHAP importances. Duration is deliberately excluded: it is partly an outcome of company.
- `data/episodes.parquet` — the derived episode table (48,258 fitness episodes plus sport/other). Raw ATUS files are git-ignored; `download.py` refetches them.
- `output/` — yearly and era tables, by-activity and demographic cuts, gym table, decomposition, model metrics.

Reproduce: `pip install -r requirements.txt && python src/download.py && python src/analyze.py && python src/model.py && python src/charts.py`.

## Caveats, stated flatly

"Alone" in ATUS means no *known* companion was recorded: a spin class full of strangers, or a gym floor full of regulars you did not name, codes as alone. That is a real limitation and also partly the point — the data measures exercise with *your people*, not exercise near people — but a class-based social workout the respondent experienced socially can land in the solo bucket. ATUS response rates fell after 2020; the weights re-balance on demographics, and the age pattern (the young moved, the old did not) is hard to explain as pure nonresponse, but composition of who answers diaries is a live alternative explanation I cannot fully kill. Each respondent gives one diary day, so nothing here follows a person over time, and the friend-duration premium is descriptive: long activities recruit company as surely as company extends activities. Walking (130131) is the single biggest code and includes casual walking reported as recreation; the fitness set is my judgment call, which is why the sport and broad sets are reported alongside it. Finally, 2025's friend-share uptick is one year of a noisy series.
