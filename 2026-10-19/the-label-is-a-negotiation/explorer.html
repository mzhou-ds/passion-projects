The Label Is a Negotiation

Musing with Mike · daily build #43 · A Nutrition Facts panel is not a measurement. It is the output of three negotiated numbers: what is in the food, how big a serving the maker chose, and the FDA rounding grid the result is snapped to. Set the first two and watch the third decide what you get to read.

Simplified 21 CFR 101.9(c) rounding: calories <5 print as 0, then to the nearest 5 (to 50) or 10; fats <0.5 g print as 0, then to the nearest 0.5 g (to 5 g) or 1 g; sodium <5 mg prints as 0, then to the nearest 5 mg (to 140 mg) or 10 mg; cholesterol <2 mg prints as 0, then to the nearest 5 mg; sugars and fiber <0.5 g print as 0, then to the nearest gram. The defaults above are a breath mint: 95% sugar, served at 0.49 g.

The census behind this page

Across 629,114 current US branded products (USDA FoodData Central, April 2025), 45,948 product–nutrient pairs print a 0 while the manufacturer's own per-100 g submission shows the nutrient is there. Relabeling every product at its own category's median serving kills 16,680 of those zeros and 82,183 "low" claim eligibilities. The serving is not describing the food. In about 99,000 cases, the serving is the label. Full method and findings in the README.
