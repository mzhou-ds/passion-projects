DRAFT - Musing with Mike - The Cousin Cliff

Fertility fell about 20 percent in a generation. Cousins fell by more than 70 percent. Those two facts do not contradict each other. They multiply.

Your cousins are a product, not a rate. Aunts and uncles times the kids each of them had. Cut both numbers and the result falls much faster than either one.

So I built the expected family tree for babies born from 1979 to 2023 in the US, Canada, and China, using UN fertility data.

An American baby born in 1990 expected about 11 first cousins. That was the peak, and it came a generation after the baby boom, because the boom's kids inherited all those siblings as aunts and uncles.

A baby born in 2023 expects about 3. Canada is under 2. China is close to 1.

The steepest drop happened between 1990 and 2000, while US fertility sat near 2 and everyone said births had stabilized. The leg that collapsed was not births that decade. It was aunts and uncles, set by the fertility bust twenty years earlier.

Then there is the case averages hide. If both parents are only children, common for urban Chinese families of the one-child generation, a kid gets zero aunts, zero uncles, and zero first cousins, whatever country they are born in. The family tree stops being a bush and becomes a line.

Kin is infrastructure. Childcare help, elder care split five ways, a couch in another city. When the tree shrinks by half in a generation, all of that has to be bought, built, or done without.
