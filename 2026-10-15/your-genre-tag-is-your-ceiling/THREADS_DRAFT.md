Your genre tag is your ceiling.

I pulled every chart Beatport promotes today: the overall Top 100 plus all 17 genre Top 100s. 1,700 tracks. The number of tracks charting in two genres at once is zero. Every track gets exactly one tag, and the tag decides the ceiling.

A House track has a 30% shot at the overall Top 100. Tech House, 20%. A Mainstage or Techno track at the top of its own chart: 1%. Half the overall chart is just House and Tech House. It is a house chart with guests.

Tracks cannot cross shelves. Artists can. Chris Lake and Tiga each chart in six genres today, with different tracks tagged for different rooms. Tracks by three-genre artists cross over at 15.9%, against 3.5% for single-genre artists.

And the remix does not save you. Tracks with remixers cross at 2.8%, less than half the rate of tracks without one. Even when the remixer is the bigger name.

The tag is chosen at upload, before anyone hears the record. It might be the most expensive metadata field in dance music.
