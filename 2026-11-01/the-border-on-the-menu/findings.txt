THE BORDER ON THE MENU — findings (2026-11-01)
35,619 restaurants in 16 city cores (8 Canada, 8 US), 26,972 with a cuisine tag (75.7%).
Source: OpenStreetMap cuisine tags, via BBBike city extracts and Geofabrik
province extracts (Overpass was unusable on 2026-11-01: all four mirrors timed
out even on a tiny test query).

1. You can taste the border. A logistic regression on cuisine shares picks the
   right country for all 16 cities in leave-one-city-out validation (16/16).
   Shuffled labels average 44.5%; permutation p = 0.0005.

2. Menus jump at the border, they do not drift with distance. Mean cuisine
   distance is 0.077 within a country and 0.142 across it. In a regression on
   120 city pairs, the cross-border coefficient is +0.065 (permutation
   p < 0.0002) while a standard deviation of kilometres adds only +0.006.

3. Vancouver and Seattle are the proof. They are 191 km apart with menu
   distance 0.148. Portland and Seattle are 234 km apart with distance 0.017.
   Boston to New York is 302 km at 0.016. Vancouver's top cuisine is Japanese
   (21.4% of tagged restaurants); Seattle's is Italian (14.6%), then American
   and Mexican. Same coast, different menu.

4. The border is a wall for some cuisines and a sieve for others. Mexican
   food: 3.2% of Canadian tagged restaurants vs 11.4% in the US (0.28x).
   Indian (1.95x) and Vietnamese (1.80x) lean Canadian. Italian is identical
   on both sides (15.7% vs 16.0%, 0.98x). Even "American" food leans south
   (8.2% vs 13.7%, 0.60x).

5. Two hypotheses died. Canadian menus are NOT more diverse: mean cuisine
   entropy 3.70 in Canada vs 3.76 in the US. And Chinese menus are NOT more
   regionally specific north of the border: only 4.7% of Canadian
   Chinese-family restaurants carry a regional tag (Sichuan, Cantonese,
   dim sum) vs 5.8% in the US.

6. The national dish is a rounding error. "Canadian" cuisine tags appear on
   0.68% of Canadian tagged restaurants and 0.01% of American ones. French
   tags are as common in US cities (1.22%) as Canadian ones (0.99%): Montreal
   does not make French food Canadian.

Caveats: OSM tags are crowd-mapped, so coverage varies (61% Halifax to 86%
Seattle) and all shares are among tagged restaurants. City cores are fixed
bboxes, not metro areas, so this is downtown menus, not whole metros.
