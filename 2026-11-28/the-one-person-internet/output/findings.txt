THE ONE-PERSON INTERNET
Build #81 — 2026-11-28 — tech-industry
A download-weighted census of who maintains npm, the registry every
JavaScript app on earth installs from.

HEADLINE
53.1% of all downloads in npm's top 1,000 packages flow through packages
maintained by a single person. 538 of the top 1,000 packages have exactly
one maintainer; the median solo-maintained package is downloaded 117 million
times a week. The internet does not run on institutions. It runs on
individuals, mostly unpaid, mostly alone.

THE ZOMBIE CORE
25.9% of top-1,000 downloads flow through packages nobody has published in
24+ months. 299 of the top 1,000 are "abandoned" by that line, including:
  ms                    727M/week, last publish Dec 2020 (6 maintainers)
  json-schema-traverse  464M/week, last publish Dec 2020 (1 maintainer)
  safe-buffer           451M/week, last publish May 2020 (2 maintainers)
  path-key              407M/week, last publish Apr 2021 (1 maintainer)
  has-flag              398M/week, last publish Jul 2021 (1 maintainer)
  isarray               347M/week, last publish Jul 2019 (1 maintainer)
16.5% of all top-1,000 downloads flow through packages that are BOTH solo
maintained AND untouched for 2+ years. Caveat: many of these are finished,
not broken. A 20-line utility can be done. The risk is not rot, it is that
nobody is watching: a compromised publish to any of them ships silently
into hundreds of millions of installs a week. That is the xz attack surface,
measured.

ABANDONMENT IS A SOLO-MAINTAINER PROBLEM
Share of packages with no publish in 24 months, by maintainer count:
  1 maintainer:  36.3% abandoned; 31.0% of the bucket's downloads are abandoned
  2 maintainers: 29.1%; 24.8% of downloads
  3-5:           18.7%; 17.9% of downloads
  6-10:          15.4%; 16.4% of downloads
  11+:            0.0%; 0.0% of downloads
Logistic regression (n=1,000, McFadden R2 0.077): each additional maintainer
cuts the odds of abandonment by 21% (odds ratio 0.79, SE 0.06). Scoped
(org-owned) packages are far safer (OR 0.23). At median downloads, a solo
package has a 41% predicted chance of being abandoned vs 21% with five
maintainers. Honest limit: the model is explanatory, not predictive. Accuracy
70.1% equals the majority-class baseline, so abandonment stays mostly
idiosyncratic. Maintainer count moves the odds, it does not decide them.

CONCENTRATION
The registry looks decentralised by package count (27,315 packages in our
pool, millions overall). By downloads it is not. Of top-1,000 downloads:
top 10 packages 5.2%, top 50 17.3%, top 100 27.7%, top 250 49.7%,
top 500 73.4%. The top 250 packages carry half of everything.

BLAST RADIUS
Even the biggest packages sit on solo maintainers. Of the top 200 packages'
264 direct dependency edges, 56.9% point at single-maintainer packages and
25.2% point at abandoned ones. 65 of the top 200 (32.5%) have at least one
solo-maintained direct dependency; 36 (18%) have at least one abandoned
direct dependency. 39 of the top 200 depend ENTIRELY on solo maintainers
(every direct dep solo), including side-channel (5/5), browserslist (5/5),
ajv (3/3) and resolve (4/4). 113 of the top 200 have no direct dependencies
at all, which is itself the point: the core is tiny utilities, and tiny
utilities have one author.

SO WHAT (the operator read)
npm is a two-sided marketplace whose supply side is almost entirely
unpaid individuals, and its most-used inventory is the least watched.
Three things this prices in:
1. If you run a platform, your supply-chain risk is not your direct deps,
   it is the solo maintainers two levels down. Audit by maintainer count,
   not just by CVE.
2. The cheapest insurance in software is paying the maintainer of the
   20-line package your whole stack stands on. A $500/month sponsorship
   to the right 50 people buys more security than most tooling budgets.
3. "Abandoned" is the wrong word for most of the zombie core. They are
   finished. The fix is not more publishes, it is more eyes: shared
   maintenance, org adoption, or a bus-factor floor for the top 250.

METHOD (one paragraph)
Pool built 2026-11-28 from the npm registry search API: 131 broad queries,
27,315 unique packages, each with weekly downloads, dependent count, last
publish date and maintainer list. Validated against 100+ known giants;
every missing one recovered by exact lookup. Top 1,000 ranked by weekly
downloads (161.6B/week). Dependency lists from abbreviated registry
metadata for the top 200; all 197 unique direct deps resolved except two
phantom entries (@jest/react-is-18/19) that 404 on the registry.
Abandoned = no publish in 730 days. Full scripts in src/.
