I measured how fast Chinese dies in Canada.

First-gen Chinese Canadians with a Chinese mother tongue: 91.5%.
Second gen: 51.5%.
Third gen: 5.8%.

The language has a half-life of about 14 years. The only thing refilling it is immigration — and at current rates, the tap doesn't cover the drain. Holding the market flat would take ~66,000 net new Chinese-mother-tongue immigrants a year.

Two wrinkles. Cantonese transmits at 80% into the second generation; Mandarin manages 41%. Mandarin won on volume, not loyalty. And in Edmonton — where second-gen adults my age still run 10:1 Cantonese — the kids under 15 already go 2.4:1 Mandarin. The stock is a lagging indicator. The flip already happened; the totals just haven't noticed.

I'm second gen. Keeping the language is a coin flip — Edmonton's second generation retained it at 49%. I didn't, and now I'm re-learning Chinese as an adult in San Francisco. The census has a row for my family.

The market decaying on schedule is 1.35M speakers. The market compounding quietly is 263k of us who lost the language and kept the identity.
