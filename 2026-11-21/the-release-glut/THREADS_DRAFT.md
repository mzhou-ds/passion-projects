Electronic music doubled its output in 15 years. The median release now has one collector. One.

I measured the whole Discogs catalog: 97,657 electronic releases in 2005, 199,047 in 2020, then it stopped growing. Pop stayed flat at 51k a year the whole time. The glut is real, but it saturated in 2020, the lockdown year.

The audience per release fell off a cliff: median collectors 16 in 2005, 3 in 2015, 1 in 2025. A third of 2025's releases are owned by nobody at all.

Here's the mechanism, and it's not what I expected. It's the format. Within every single year, a vinyl release has a median of 47 to 83 collectors. A file release has a median of 1. The shelf didn't lose its audience. It stopped being collectable.

The labels dissolved too. Top 10 labels hold 6% of releases. The biggest "label" in the catalog is literally "Not On Label." There are no gatekeepers left, just a long tail and a pressing plant.

And the gate at the top got narrower while the shelf got wider: 92% of today's Beatport Top 100 sits in a 12-BPM corridor, median 129 BPM, and half the chart is less than a month old.
