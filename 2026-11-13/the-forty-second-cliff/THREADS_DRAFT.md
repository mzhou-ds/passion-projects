The average NHL shift lasts 48 seconds. The cliff starts at 40.

I joined every shift chart to every shot from the full 2025-26 season, all 1,312 games. A line 10 to 30 seconds into its shift concedes 3 expected goals per 60. Past 60 seconds, it concedes 16 to 19. Forty-plus seconds is 15% of the ice time and 37% of the goals against.

And it is not just "they only get stuck out there because they're trapped." When both lines are equally old, long continuous play with nobody able to change, the cliff is 14x, not 4x. When the attackers are fresh and cycling in, the classic siege, it is 3x. Fatigue is doing the work.

The other surprise: changing is safe. Change two skaters on the fly and you concede 2.2 expected goals per 60, the safest state I measured. Change all five at once and it is 17.6, the most dangerous. The full line change is the problem, not the change.

McDavid averages a 61-second shift, fifth longest in the league. The stars take the longest shifts, and those extra seconds are the most expensive minutes in hockey. The rule is not "keep shifts under 45." It is get off at 30, and never let a line see 40.
