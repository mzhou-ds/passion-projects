Streaming didn't shorten EDM. It deleted the other version.

Dance music used to ship every song twice: a radio cut and a club cut. Strobe was 3:32 on radio and 10:37 in full. One More Time came at 3:55, 5:20, and 8:00.

I measured the spread between the shortest and longest cut of 8,531 songs in the storefront catalog. Songs with a real second length: 20% in the 90s, still 17% in 2010-14, then 7% now. The radio-plus-club pair held flat for 20 years and died in about three, right when playlists replaced downloads. A playlist slot is one slot. A second version stopped being a second product and became competition for your own song.

Remixes survived, they just stopped adding length. Silence got six remixes in 2017 and every one lands within 53 seconds of the original.

And the tracks still labeled Extended? I measured their openings. They start at full brightness, no DJ intro at all. The word survived the thing it named.
